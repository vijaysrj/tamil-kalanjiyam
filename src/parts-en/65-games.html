Games and sports

Martial arts, bull-embracing, village festival contests, street games, board games played on the verandah, and on to today's cricket and chess.

Play in the Sangam age

  • Men: wrestling, practice with bow, sword and spear, bull-embracing, throwing discs, water sports.
  • Women: kazhangu (tossing and catching seeds), ammanai, ball games, the swing, orai, building sand houses.
  • The Kalithokai sings that the herders of the forest land gave their daughter in marriage only to the man who subdued the bull.

Martial arts

  • Silambam: fighting with a whirling bamboo staff. Footwork, strikes, blocks and display spins are learned in stages. Double sticks, deer horns (madu), the flexible sword (surul vaal) and fire torches are its branches. It is now taught in schools and counts in Tamil Nadu's sports quota.
  • Kuthu varisai: unarmed fighting with hands and feet.
  • Varma kalai: knowledge of the body's vital points, used both to strike and to heal. A tradition of masters (asans) in Kanyakumari district.
  • Adimurai: the fighting system of the far south.
  • Wrestling and the valari: the valari, a curved throwing weapon, was used in war and hunting in the Poligar period.

Bull-embracing (eru thazhuvuthal)

Also called jallikattu or manjuvirattu. It takes place around Pongal. A bull charges out of the gate (vadivasal) and a man must cling to its hump for a set distance. The bull must not be hurt; there are no weapons. Honour goes to the bull that cannot be held.

  • Famous venues: Avaniyapuram (Thai Pongal day), Palamedu (Mattu Pongal), Alanganallur (Kaanum Pongal); all three in Madurai district.
  • Forms: the vadivasal jallikattu; manjuvirattu, where bulls run in the open; vadamadu, where a team tackles a bull on a long rope; the eruthu vidum vizha of the northern districts.
  • Native breeds: Kangayam, Pulikulam, Umblachery, Bargur. Supporters argue the sport is what keeps these breeds alive.
  • In the courts: the Supreme Court banned it in 2014 on animal-welfare grounds. In January 2017 a vast peaceful protest filled the Marina in Chennai and towns across the state; the state amended the law. In 2023 a Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court upheld the amendment. It is now held under safety rules.

Rekla: bullock-cart racing, popular in the Kongu and southern districts.

Village festival games

  • Sadugudu (kabaddi): a raider enters the other side chanting "kabaddi, kabaddi" on a single breath, tags someone and tries to return; the defenders try to hold him. The state game of Tamil Nadu.
  • Uriyadi: blindfolded, break a swinging pot with a stick.
  • Vazhukku maram: climb a tall greased pole to take the prize at the top.
  • Ilavattakkal: lift a heavy round stone and throw it over the shoulder; once the proof of a young man's strength.
  • Tug of war, silambam bouts, cart races, kolam contests.

Board games

Pallanguzhi

For two players. The board has fourteen pits, seven to a row. Each pit holds five or six counters (tamarind seeds, cowries, red seeds); the number varies by region.

  • Lift the counters from one pit on your side and drop them one by one into the pits that follow.
  • When your hand is empty, lift the counters from the next pit and carry on.
  • If the next pit is empty, you win everything in the pit beyond it, and the turn passes.
  • If a pit reaches four counters while sowing, that is a "pasu" (cow) and its owner takes it.
  • A round ends when the counters run out. Each player refills their pits from their winnings; a pit that cannot be filled is closed for the next round. The game ends when a player cannot fill even one pit.

It trains a child to count, calculate and plan ahead.

Aadu puli aattam (goats and tigers)

Played by two on a triangular grid. One has three tigers, the other fifteen goats. A tiger kills a goat by jumping over it. If the goats together hem in the tigers so they cannot move, the goats win. The lesson: the strong can be beaten by cooperation. The board can be seen carved into the floors of temple halls.

Thaayam

For two to four players on a square grid. Brass dice or cowries are thrown and pieces move by the number that falls. A piece enters the board only on a "thaayam" (one). Pieces can be captured, except on the crossed safe squares. The first to bring all pieces to the centre wins.

Paramapadam, chess and others

  • Paramapadam: snakes and ladders, by tradition played through the night of Vaikunta Ekadasi. Good deeds are ladders, bad deeds snakes.
  • Chathurangam (chess): at Thiruppoovanur in Thiruvarur district there is a Siva temple whose deity is named Chathuranga Vallabhanathar, "lord of chess".
  • Grid games: natchathira vilaiyattu, thattangal, sokkattan.

Sport today

Chess

  • Manuel Aaron, India's first International Master, grew up in Chennai.
  • Viswanathan Anand: India's first grandmaster (1988); five-time world champion.
  • D. Gukesh: world champion in 2024 at eighteen, the youngest in history.
  • R. Praggnanandhaa and his sister R. Vaishali are both grandmasters.
  • The 44th Chess Olympiad was held at Mamallapuram in 2022.

Cricket

  • The M.A. Chidambaram Stadium at Chepauk, Chennai, is one of India's oldest Test grounds, known for crowds that understand and applaud the game.
  • From Tamil Nadu to the Indian team: S. Venkataraghavan, K. Srikkanth, R. Ashwin, Dinesh Karthik, Washington Sundar, T. Natarajan.
  • The IPL side Chennai Super Kings; tennis-ball cricket in every street.
  • Muttiah Muralitharan, who took 800 Test wickets, is a Hill Country Tamil of Sri Lanka.

Other sports

  • Hockey: Vasudevan Baskaran captained the Indian team that won gold at the 1980 Moscow Olympics.
  • Athletics: Mariyappan Thangavelu won high-jump gold at the 2016 Paralympics.
  • Tennis: Ramanathan Krishnan, Ramesh Krishnan, Vijay Amritraj.
  • Squash, table tennis: Joshna Chinappa, Dipika Pallikal; Sharath Kamal.
  • Fencing: Bhavani Devi, the first Indian fencer at the Olympics.
  • Motor racing: Narain Karthikeyan of Coimbatore, the first Indian to drive in Formula 1.
  • Carrom: the street game of North Chennai; Maria Irudayam was world champion.
  • Kabaddi, volleyball, football: teams in every village; Tamil Thalaivas in Pro Kabaddi.

At the annual Tamil sports days of the diaspora, kabaddi, kilithattu (a traditional game of Eelam), tug of war and uriyadi bring the community together.

Rules vary from village to village. Those given here are the common form; ask the elders of your own place and record the local rules too.
