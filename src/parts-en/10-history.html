History

The Tamils are a people of the far south of India and the north and east of Sri Lanka, with an unbroken written tradition of more than two and a half thousand years. Their story begins in the Stone Age and today spans the world.

Prehistory

  • About 1.5 million years ago
    Hand-axes used by Old Stone Age people have been found at Attirampakkam near Chennai, among the oldest traces of humans in India.
  • Neolithic and Iron Age
    Farming and herding began at sites such as Paiyampalli. The megalithic culture followed: the dead were buried in large urns, with stone cists, stone circles and hero stones. Adichanallur, Sivagalai and Kodumanal are key sites.
  • The antiquity of iron
    In 2025 the Tamil Nadu Department of Archaeology reported, from samples at Sivagalai in Thoothukudi district, that iron was in use in Tamil Nadu as early as the start of the fourth millennium BCE. That would be among the earliest dates in the world. Scholarly review of the claim continues.
  • From the 6th century BCE: Keezhadi
    At Keezhadi on the Vaigai near Madurai, excavators found brick structures, ring wells, drains, weaving tools, ornaments and potsherds inscribed in Tamili (Tamil-Brahmi). It is archaeological evidence for the urban life described in Sangam poetry.

The Sangam age (c. 300 BCE – 300 CE)

The age of the three crowned kings, the Chera, Chola and Pandya, and of lesser chieftains (Velir) such as Pari and Athiyaman. Ashoka's inscriptions (3rd century BCE) name the Chola, Pandya, Keralaputra and Satiyaputra lands as neighbours outside his rule.

  • Capitals: Vanji (Karur) for the Cheras; Uraiyur and Puhar for the Cholas; Madurai for the Pandyas. Emblems: bow, tiger, fish.
  • Famous kings: Karikala Chola, builder of the Kallanai; Nedunchezhiyan, victor of Talaiyalanganam; Cheran Senguttuvan, who raised a temple to Kannagi.
  • The seven patrons: Pari, Ori, Kari, Athiyaman, Pegan, Ay and Nalli. Pari, who gave his chariot to a jasmine creeper, and Athiyaman, who gave the poet Avvaiyar a rare gooseberry, are bywords for generosity.
  • Sea trade: From the ports of Muziris, Tondi, Korkai, Puhar and Arikamedu, pepper, pearls, gems and cloth went to Rome, Egypt and Southeast Asia. Hoards of Roman coins have been found in Tamil Nadu.
  • Society: Poets, bards and dancers were honoured at court. More than thirty women poets, among them Avvaiyar, Velliveethiyar and Kakkaipadiniyar, are represented.
On the Sangams and Kumari Kandam: Tradition, recorded in the commentary on Iraiyanar Akapporul, says there were three Tamil academies and that the first two stood on a southern land lost to the sea. This is a legend deeply rooted in Tamil memory, but there is no geological or archaeological evidence for it. The surviving Sangam literature is historical fact.

Kalabhras, Pallavas and early Pandyas (300 – 850 CE)

  • 3rd – 6th century
    Kalabhra rule. Once called a "dark age" because evidence is thin, yet Jainism and Buddhism flourished, and the Tirukkural, other ethical works, the Silappatikaram and the Manimekalai belong to about this time.
  • 6th – 9th century
    Pallavas ruled from Kanchipuram and Pandyas from Madurai. Mahendravarman began rock-cut temples. Narasimhavarman (Mamallan) took Vatapi; the monuments of Mamallapuram date from his reign.
  • The Bhakti movement
    The sixty-three Nayanmars and twelve Alvars travelled from town to town singing in Tamil, the language of the people. The idea that devotion belongs to everyone later spread across India.

The imperial Cholas (850 – 1279)

  • c. 850
    Vijayalaya Chola captured Thanjavur.
  • c. 920
    The Uthiramerur inscription of Parantaka I records rules for choosing village assembly members by lot (the kudavolai system).
  • 985 – 1014: Rajaraja I
    Rule over South India, northern Sri Lanka and the Maldives. Land survey, a tax system and a strong navy. The great temple at Thanjavur was completed in 1010.
  • 1014 – 1044: Rajendra I
    An expedition to the Ganga; a new capital at Gangaikonda Cholapuram. In 1025 his fleet crossed the sea to attack Srivijaya (Kadaram).
  • Culture
    Bronzes such as the Nataraja, the Kamba Ramayanam, the Periya Puranam and thousands of inscriptions. Merchant guilds such as the Ainnurruvar and Manigramam traded as far as Southeast Asia and China.

Later Pandyas to Nayaks and Marathas (1200 – 1800)

  • 13th century
    The Pandyas peaked under Jatavarman Sundara Pandyan. Marco Polo recorded the pearl and horse trade of the Pandya country.
  • 1311
    Malik Kafur's invasion, followed by the Madurai Sultanate (1335 – 1378).
  • 14th – 17th century
    Nayaks of Madurai, Thanjavur and Gingee under the Vijayanagara empire. The palayakkarar (poligar) system. Tirumalai Nayak and Rani Mangammal. The Madurai Meenakshi temple took its present form.
  • From 1674
    Maratha rule at Thanjavur. Serfoji II built up the Saraswati Mahal Library.
  • Islam and Christianity
    Islam spread along the coast through Arab traders (Kayalpattinam, Kilakarai, Nagore). Christianity spread with the Europeans. In 1578 "Thambiran Vanakkam" was printed, the first book printed in an Indian script.

European rule and resistance (1600 – 1947)

  • Settlements
    Portuguese, Dutch, Danes at Tranquebar (1620), English at Madras (1639), French at Pondicherry (1674).
  • The Poligar wars
    Puli Thevar; Velu Nachiyar, who won back Sivaganga (1780); Veerapandiya Kattabomman (hanged 1799); the Marudhu brothers (1801); Dheeran Chinnamalai (1805). The Vellore mutiny of 1806.
  • Madras Presidency
    English education, printing, railways. In 1856 Caldwell established the Dravidian language family. U.V. Swaminatha Iyer and C.W. Thamotharampillai hunted down palm-leaf manuscripts and printed them, which is how Sangam literature was recovered.
  • The freedom struggle
    V.O. Chidambaram's Swadeshi shipping company (1906); Bharati's songs; Subramania Siva; Vanchinathan (1911); the Vedaranyam salt march (1930); Tiruppur Kumaran, who died holding the flag (1932); Kamaraj.
  • The social justice movement
    The Dalit voices of Iyothee Thass and Rettamalai Srinivasan; the Justice Party (1916); Periyar E.V. Ramasamy's Self-Respect Movement (1925), which stood for the abolition of caste, women's freedom and rationalism. The first agitation against compulsory Hindi came in 1937.

Tamil Nadu after independence

  • 1949
    C.N. Annadurai founded the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (DMK).
  • 1954 – 1963
    Kamaraj's government: a school in every village, midday meals, dams, industrial estates.
  • 1956
    States were reorganised by language. Kanyakumari joined the state.
  • 1965
    The anti-Hindi agitation. The two-language policy (Tamil and English) took hold in Tamil Nadu.
  • 1967 – 1969
    The DMK came to power; self-respect marriages were made legal; on 14 January 1969 Madras State was renamed Tamil Nadu.
  • 1969 – 2021
    The two Dravidian parties alternated in power under M. Karunanidhi (DMK) and M.G. Ramachandran and J. Jayalalithaa (AIADMK). Nutritious meals, reservations, public distribution, girls' education and industrial growth mark the period.
  • 2004
    India declared Tamil a classical language. That December the tsunami struck the coast.
  • 2026
    In the assembly election the Tamilaga Vettri Kazhagam, founded by the actor Vijay, became the largest party and formed the government with coalition support, ending the two-party alternation that had lasted since 1967.

The Tamils of Eelam (Sri Lanka)

Tamils have lived in the north and east of Sri Lanka since ancient times. The Jaffna kingdom lasted from the 13th century until the Portuguese conquest of 1619. The Hill Country (Malaiyaha) Tamils descend from workers the British brought from Tamil Nadu to the tea estates in the 19th century.

  • 1948
    Independence. Most Hill Country Tamils lost their citizenship.
  • 1956
    The "Sinhala Only" Act. Tamil rights campaigns began.
  • 1981
    The Jaffna Public Library, with about 97,000 books and rare manuscripts, was burned.
  • 1983 – 2009
    After the anti-Tamil riots of July 1983, civil war between the state and Tamil militant groups. The war ended at Mullivaikkal in May 2009. Tens of thousands of people died and hundreds of thousands were displaced.

Tamils around the world

Those taken under British and French rule as plantation labour to Malaysia, Singapore, South Africa, Mauritius, Réunion, Fiji and the Caribbean, and those who left in the twentieth century for study, work or refuge in Canada, Britain, Europe, Australia, the United States and the Gulf, together make the Tamils a global community. Details are in the chapter "The Tamil world".
