Faith and philosophy

The Tamils are not people of one religion. Hindus, Christians, Muslims, Jains, Buddhists and atheists are all Tamils. What joins them is the language.

Early worship

  • Hero stones (nadukal): a stone raised and worshipped for a warrior who died in battle or defending cattle; the beginning of ancestor worship.
  • Gods of the landscapes: Seyon (Murugan), Mayon, Vendan, Varunan, Kotravai. The frenzied dance of the Velan priest and the kuravai dance were Sangam-age rites.
  • Murugan: hailed as the "Tamil god". His six abodes: Thirupparankundram, Thiruchendur, Palani, Swamimalai, Thiruthani, Pazhamudircholai.

Folk deities

This is the everyday religion of village Tamils. Ayyanar on horseback at the village boundary; Mariamman, who brings rain and cures disease; guardian gods such as Karuppasami, Muniyandi, Sudalaimadan and Madurai Veeran; Isakki, Pechi, Kali. Every family has a kuladeivam (family deity), and returns once a year to the native village to cook pongal and worship. The priests are mostly from the community itself. Fire-walking, milk-pot processions, sprouted-grain offerings and animal sacrifice are forms of worship.

Saivism

  • Siva as the supreme. The twelve Tirumurai sung by the Nayanmars are the basic texts.
  • Saiva Siddhanta: the doctrine of three realities: pati (God), pasu (soul), pasam (bondage). Meykandar's Sivagnanabotham is its first text.
  • Shrines: Chidambaram (Nataraja), Madurai, Thiruvannamalai, Thanjavur, Rameswaram, Thiruvarur; the five-element shrines; in Sri Lanka, Thiruketheeswaram, Thirukoneswaram and Nallur.
  • The monasteries of Thiruvavaduthurai, Dharmapuram and Thiruppanandal sustained Tamil learning.

Vaishnavism

  • Tirumal (Vishnu) as the supreme. The Alvars' Nalayira Divya Prabandham is the "Tamil Veda".
  • Ramanuja (11th – 12th century) founded Visishtadvaita and gave Tamil hymns a place in temple worship.
  • Shrines: the 108 Divya Desams. Srirangam, Kanchipuram, Srivilliputhur, Thiruvallikeni, Azhagarkoil.

Jainism and Buddhism

Jains and Buddhists lived in the Tamil country from before the Common Era. The oldest Tamili inscriptions are on cave beds given to Jain monks. Naladiyar, Seevaka Chinthamani, Nannul and the nigandus are Jain gifts; Manimekalai and Veerasoliyam are Buddhist. The very word for school, palli, comes from the Jain palli. Tamil Jains still live around Thiruvannamalai and Villupuram. In the twentieth century Iyothee Thass revived Tamil Buddhism.

Islam

It reached the coast through Arab traders as early as the seventh and eighth centuries. Tamil Muslims (Marakkayar, Labbai, Rowther) have Tamil as their mother tongue. The Seerapuranam, the songs of Masthan Sahib and the music of Nagore Hanifa are among their gifts. People of all faiths visit the dargahs of Nagore and Ervadi. There was also a tradition, Arwi, of writing Tamil in Arabic script.

Christianity

Tradition holds that Saint Thomas died at Mylapore in Chennai. From the 16th century the Catholic and later the Protestant churches spread. The missionaries Beschi (Veeramamunivar), Ziegenbalg, Caldwell and G.U. Pope served Tamil through printing, dictionaries, linguistics and translation. Velankanni, San Thome in Chennai and Poondi are major shrines. Tamil customs such as the chariot, the flag-hoisting and Pongal have entered Christian worship as well.

The Siddhars and Vallalar

  • The Siddhars went beyond temple, ritual and caste and saw the body itself as the temple. "The heart is the great shrine, the body of flesh the temple," said Tirumular. Yoga, medicine and alchemy were their fields.
  • Ramalinga Vallalar (1823 – 1874): a path of harmony beyond creeds; compassion for all life; God as the light of grace. The hearth he lit at Vadalur in 1867 has never gone out and still feeds the hungry.

The rationalist tradition

Questioning God and religion is also part of the Tamil tradition. Sangam literature is largely worldly. The Manimekalai sets many creeds debating face to face. In the twentieth century Periyar opposed superstition, caste and patriarchy and made atheism a mass movement. Today it is normal for the devout and the rationalist to share one family and one stage.

The temple as a social institution

In the Tamil country a temple is not only a place of worship. Under the Cholas it was landowner, bank, school, hospital, arts centre and archive. The inscriptions on its walls are today's historical record. The temple at the centre, chariot streets around it and a tank: that is the plan of the traditional town (Madurai, Srirangam). The temple-entry struggles (Vaikom 1924, Madurai 1939) opened temples to everyone.
