Arts

Music, dance and theatre, sculpture, architecture, weaving, cinema. Tamil art is the classical and the folk together.

Music

  • Pann music: in the Sangam age each landscape had its own mode (pann); the yazh (harp), flute and drum. The Silappatikaram describes the grammar of music and dance at length. The Thevaram is still sung to its panns by temple singers (othuvars).
  • Carnatic music: Thanjavur is its cradle. The Tamil trinity: Muthu Thandavar, Arunachala Kavirayar, Marimutha Pillai. Songs of Gopalakrishna Bharati, Papanasam Sivan and Bharati. The December music season in Chennai.
  • Instruments: nadaswaram and thavil (auspicious music; no ceremony is without them); veena, mridangam, ghatam, flute; parai, urumi, pambai, udukkai.
  • Parai: the oldest Tamil drum. Once confined to funerals, it now sounds on stage as a symbol of liberation.
  • Folk music: lullaby, lament, themmangu, kummi, villuppattu (bow-song), gaana (Chennai).
  • Film music: G. Ramanathan, K.V. Mahadevan, M.S. Viswanathan, Ilaiyaraaja, A.R. Rahman. This is the shared musical language of Tamils today.

Dance and theatre

  • Bharatanatyam: sadir, danced in temples and courts by the devadasi community, was reshaped as a stage art in the twentieth century. The Thanjavur Quartet laid out its repertoire.
  • Therukoothu: all-night open-air theatre of Mahabharata stories; part of the Draupadi Amman festival in the northern districts.
  • Folk dances: karakattam, kavadiattam, mayilattam, oyilattam, devarattam, poikkal kuthirai (dummy horse), puliyattam (tiger dance), kolattam, kummi, thappattam, puppetry and shadow puppets.
  • Drama: Sankaradas Swamigal, Pammal Sambandha Mudaliar; the boys' companies; Dravidian movement plays; modern theatre (Na. Muthuswamy's Koothu-p-pattarai).

Architecture and sculpture

  • 7th – 8th century: Pallavas
    Rock-cut caves, monolithic rathas and the Shore Temple (Mamallapuram); the Kailasanatha temple at Kanchi.
  • 10th – 12th century: Cholas
    The great temple at Thanjavur (tower about 60 metres), Gangaikonda Cholapuram, Darasuram. The three are UNESCO World Heritage as the "Great Living Chola Temples".
  • Bronzes
    Images of Nataraja, Uma and Rama cast by the lost-wax method. The sthapatis of Swamimalai still cast the same way.
  • 13th – 17th century: Pandyas, Nayaks
    Soaring gopurams, thousand-pillared halls, long corridors: Madurai, Srirangam, Rameswaram, Chidambaram.
  • Others
    The forts of Gingee and Vellore; Tirumalai Nayak's palace; Chettinad mansions; the Indo-Saracenic buildings of Chennai.

Painting and crafts

  • The Jain cave paintings of Sittanavasal; Chola murals in the Thanjavur temple; Thanjavur painting with gold leaf and glass stones.
  • Kanchi silk, Thanjavur bobble-head dolls and art plates, Nachiarkoil lamps, Pattamadai mats, Manamadurai pottery, terracotta Ayyanar horses, Kallakurichi wood carving.

Cinema

In Tamil life cinema is not mere entertainment; it has shaped language, politics, dress and music.

  • 1918
    R. Nataraja Mudaliar's "Keechaka Vadham", the first silent film of South India.
  • 1931
    "Kalidas", the first Tamil talkie.
  • 1940s – 1960s
    The Dravidian movement made the screen a political platform: Anna's "Velaikkari", and "Parasakthi" (1952) with dialogue by Karunanidhi. M.G.R. and Sivaji Ganesan.
  • 1970s – 1990s
    K. Balachander, Bharathiraja (who took the camera to the village), Mahendran, Balu Mahendra, Mani Ratnam; Kamal Haasan, Rajinikanth; the music of Ilaiyaraaja.
  • Since 2000
    Bala, Vetrimaaran, Pa. Ranjith, Mari Selvaraj and others brought regional life and caste oppression to the screen. A.R. Rahman won two Oscars in 2009.

Many chief ministers of Tamil Nadu, among them Anna, Karunanidhi, M.G.R., Jayalalithaa and Vijay, came from the film world.
