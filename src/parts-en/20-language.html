Language

Tamil is the oldest literary language of the Dravidian family. A Tamil speaker today can, with a little practice, read and follow a poem written two thousand years ago. That continuity is what sets Tamil apart.

Family and spread

  • More than twenty languages, including Tamil, Malayalam, Kannada, Telugu and Tulu, belong to the Dravidian family. So does Brahui, spoken in Pakistan.
  • More than eighty million people speak Tamil.
  • It is the official language of Tamil Nadu and Puducherry and an official language of Sri Lanka and Singapore; Malaysia has Tamil-medium schools.
  • In 2004 it became the first language India declared classical.

The sound ழ (zh) is distinctive to Tamil. The Grantha letters ஜ, ஷ, ஸ, ஹ, க்ஷ are used for words borrowed from other languages.

  • From the 6th – 3rd century BCE
    Tamili (Tamil-Brahmi), seen in cave inscriptions at Mangulam, Pugalur and Jambai and on potsherds from Keezhadi and Kodumanal.
  • From the 4th century CE
    Vatteluttu; then, under the Pallavas, the forerunner of the modern Tamil script.
  • 18th century
    Veeramamunivar (Beschi) regularised the marks that distinguish short and long e and o.
  • 1978
    The script reform proposed by Periyar was adopted by the government.
  • The computer age
    Unicode Tamil, the Tamil99 keyboard, Tamil Wikipedia, machine translation.

Grammar

The oldest surviving work is the Tolkappiyam. It has three books: on letters (ezhuthu), words (sol) and subject matter (porul). Prosody (yaappu) and figures of speech (ani) were later added to make the "five grammars".

  • Letters: how sounds are produced and how they combine.
  • Words: four classes: noun, verb, particle and qualifier. A division into rational (uyarthinai) and non-rational (akrinai) classes; five genders; eight cases.
  • Subject matter: uniquely, Tamil made life itself a part of grammar. Love is akam (the interior); war, giving, rule and fame are puram (the exterior).
  • Prosody: built from letter, metrical unit, foot, linkage, line and rhyme. Four verse forms: venba, asiriyappa, kalippa, vanjippa.
  • Figures: simile, metaphor and the rest.

The three Tamils

By tradition Tamil is three things together: iyal (literature), isai (music) and naadagam (drama and dance). The language is not only written; it is sung and danced.

Spoken varieties

  • Tamil Nadu: Chennai Tamil, Kongu Tamil (Coimbatore, Erode), Madurai Tamil, Nellai Tamil, Nanjil Tamil (Kanyakumari), Thanjavur Tamil, the Chettinad dialect.
  • Sri Lanka: Jaffna Tamil, Batticaloa Tamil, Hill Country Tamil, the Tamil of Sri Lankan Muslims. Many old Tamil words survive in daily use here.
  • Elsewhere: Malaysian and Singapore Tamil; the Bangalore and Palakkad varieties.
  • Written and spoken Tamil differ clearly (diglossia). Platform speech, news and textbooks use the written form; home and cinema use the spoken.

Language movements

  • The editing movement (19th century): Arumuga Navalar, C.W. Thamotharampillai and U.V. Swaminatha Iyer brought palm-leaf texts into print.
  • The Pure Tamil movement (1916): Maraimalai Adigal called for Tamil free of Sanskrit loanwords. Parithimar Kalaignar, Devaneya Pavanar and Perunchithiranar followed. That "namaskaram" became "vanakkam" is its legacy.
  • The Tamil Isai movement (1940s): demanded a place for Tamil songs on the concert stage.
  • Opposition to compulsory Hindi (1937, 1965): language rights became the centre of Tamil politics.
  • World Tamil Conferences: the first was held in Kuala Lumpur in 1966 through the efforts of Father Thani Nayagam.
  • Scientific Tamil: coined terms such as kanini (computer), inaiyam (internet), minnanjal (email), seyali (app), perundhu (bus).
