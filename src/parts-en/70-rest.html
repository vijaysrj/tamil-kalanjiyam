Festivals

The Tamil year is measured in festivals. Farming, season, faith and history meet in each one.

Pongal: the festival of the Tamils

The first day of the month of Thai (14 or 15 January). A thanksgiving for the harvest, kept by Tamils of every faith. "When Thai is born, a way opens."

DayWhat is done
Bhogi (last day of Margazhi)Old things are cleared out and the house cleaned; protective leaves are tied at the door.
Thai PongalA kolam is drawn in the yard; a new pot, tied with fresh turmeric, is set to boil with new rice, milk and jaggery. As it boils over everyone shouts "Pongalo Pongal!". It is offered to the sun; sugarcane is eaten.
Mattu PongalThe cattle that help with the ploughing are bathed, their horns painted, and fed pongal. Bull-embracing. Thiruvalluvar Day.
Kaanum PongalVisiting relatives; gathering to eat by the river or the sea; Farmers' Day.

Other religious and public festivals

  • Muslims: Ramzan (Eid al-Fitr), Bakrid, Milad-un-Nabi; the Kanduri at Nagore. Sharing the fast-breaking gruel.
  • Christians: Christmas, Easter; the feast of Our Lady of Velankanni (29 August – 8 September).
  • The village festival: the yearly festival of the family or village goddess; chariot processions (the great chariot of Thiruvarur), float festivals, fire-walking, street theatre, a fair.
  • Public days: Thiruvalluvar Day (2 Thai), Tamil Nadu Day, Teachers' Day, the Chennai Book Fair (January).
  • Eelam Tamils: Thai Pongal, the Chithirai New Year, the Nallur Kandaswamy temple festival; 18 May, in remembrance of those who died in the war.

Knowledge and work

Water management, metallurgy, seafaring, medicine, arithmetic: traditional Tamil knowledge and its modern continuation.

Water and farming

  • Kallanai (the Grand Anicut): attributed to Karikala Chola, across the Kaveri; one of the oldest water structures in the world still in use.
  • Tanks and ponds: rainwater stored in chains of reservoirs: Veeranam, Madurantakam, Chembarambakkam. Kudimaramathu: upkeep by the villagers themselves.
  • Wet land (paddy, sugarcane, banana) and dry land (millets, pulses, cotton). The Kaveri delta is "the rice bowl of Tamil Nadu". Hundreds of heritage rice varieties: Mappillai Samba, Kichili Samba, Seeraga Samba.
  • "The world, wherever it turns, follows the plough" (Kural 1031): farming is the foremost work.

Metal and craft

  • Iron smelting is very ancient. Steel and gemstone workshops at Kodumanal. South Indian crucible steel (wootz) was exported as far as West Asia.
  • Bronze casting; ornaments of gold, pearl and coral.
  • Weaving: Sangam poems describe cloth as fine as "a snake's slough" or "smoke".

Sea and trade

  • Overseas trade riding the monsoon winds. "Cross the rolling seas to seek your fortune," said Avvaiyar.
  • The word catamaran (kattumaram, "tied logs") went from Tamil into the world's languages. Vessel types: naavaay, vangam, thoni.
  • Tamil inscriptions have been found in Thailand, Sumatra and Quanzhou in China.
  • The Nagarathar (Chettiars) ran banking as far as Burma, Malaya, Ceylon and Vietnam.

Medicine

  • Siddha medicine: the theory of three humours (vatham, pittham, kapham); pulse reading; herbal, mineral and metallic medicines. "Food is medicine." It is one of the systems recognised by the Government of India, with state Siddha medical colleges.
  • Home remedies: dry ginger, pepper, long pepper, turmeric, neem, tulsi, nilavembu; the weekly oil bath.
  • "Find the disease, find its root" (Kural 948): seek the cause before prescribing.
  • Tamil Nadu is a leading centre of modern medicine in India; patients come from across the world to hospitals in Chennai, Vellore and Coimbatore.

Arithmetic and astronomy

  • Number tables, multiplication tables and fraction tables were learned by heart in the verandah schools. Mathematical works such as the "Kanakkathikaram".
  • Land measures: kuzhi, maa, veli. The whole kingdom was surveyed in Rajaraja's time.
  • The almanac (panchangam) system for days, stars and planets; 27 lunar mansions; a 60-year cycle.

Modern scientists

  • Srinivasa Ramanujan (1887 – 1920): mathematical genius; born in Erode, raised in Kumbakonam.
  • C.V. Raman: Nobel Prize in Physics, 1930, for work on the scattering of light; born near Tiruchirappalli.
  • Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar: the evolution of stars; Nobel Prize, 1983.
  • Venki Ramakrishnan: the structure of the ribosome; Nobel Prize in Chemistry, 2009; born in Chidambaram.
  • A.P.J. Abdul Kalam: missile and space scientist; President of India; from Rameswaram.
  • M.S. Swaminathan: pioneer of India's Green Revolution.
  • Among those who led ISRO's Chandrayaan missions, Mylswamy Annadurai, K. Sivan and P. Veeramuthuvel studied in Tamil-medium schools.

The economy today

  • Chennai: automobile manufacturing ("the Detroit of Asia"), software, electronics.
  • Coimbatore: spinning mills, pumps, engineering. Tiruppur: knitwear exports. Sivakasi: fireworks, printing, matches. Hosur: vehicles, electronics. Karur: home textiles. Namakkal: eggs, lorries. Thoothukudi: port, salt.
  • Tamil Nadu has the largest number of factories, and of women factory workers, of any Indian state.

The Tamil world

Where Tamils live, and what is distinctive about each land.

Tamil Nadu

A state in south-east India. Area about 130,000 square kilometres. Population 72 million at the 2011 census. Capital: Chennai. 38 districts. The Bay of Bengal to the east, the Western Ghats to the west, Cape Comorin (Kanyakumari) to the south.

Puducherry: the Tamil-speaking union territory of Puducherry and Karaikal. Under French rule until 1954; home at various times to Bharati, Bharathidasan and Aurobindo.

State symbols

Emblem: the Srivilliputhur temple tower. Animal: Nilgiri tahr. Bird: emerald dove. Flower: glory lily. Tree: palmyra palm. Anthem: "Neerarum Kadaludutha" (from Manonmaniyam).

Sri Lanka

  • Sri Lankan Tamils: the north (Jaffna, Kilinochchi, Mullaitivu, Mannar, Vavuniya) and the east (Trincomalee, Batticaloa, Ampara). A distinct spoken Tamil, cuisine, Saiva tradition and heritage of learning.
  • Hill Country Tamils: the tea estates of Nuwara Eliya, Badulla and Kandy.
  • Tamil-speaking Muslims: a distinct ethnic identity, with Tamil as mother tongue.
  • About a quarter of Sri Lanka's people speak Tamil. It is an official language under the constitution.

The great challenge abroad is passing Tamil to the next generation. The tools are speaking Tamil at home, Tamil school, the Pongal celebration and cinema.

Learning path

If a community that knows nothing of Tamil tradition were to learn it from this treasury, generation after generation, in what order should it learn? A curriculum by age.

Culture does not come from reading alone; it comes from doing. Each stage lists what to read and what to do. The reading is in Tamil, so English readers will need to learn the language alongside or use translations. This is a proposal; each community may adapt it.

Stage 1: infancy (0 – 5 years)

  • Hear: lullabies; nursery songs such as "Nila nila odi va" and "Kai veesamma kai veesu"; grandmother's stories.
  • Do: call relatives by kin terms; say vanakkam; eat with the hand; watch the Pongal pot boil over.

Stage 2: beginning (6 – 10 years)

  • Read: the vowels, consonants and compound letters; Aathichoodi and Kondrai Vendhan (by heart); a Kural a day; Bharati's Pappa Pattu; the tales of Tenali Raman and Paramartha Guru; proverbs and riddles.
  • Do: draw a kolam; play pallanguzhi, thaayam and tops; kummi and kolattam; learn the months, days and Tamil numerals; keep a home garden.

Stage 3: middle (11 – 15 years)

  • Read: the Book of Virtue of the Tirukkural (with meaning); Moothurai, Nalvazhi, Naladiyar; the story of the Silappatikaram; selections from the Kamba Ramayanam; poems of Bharati and Bharathidasan; Kalki's Ponniyin Selvan; the History chapter of this treasury.
  • Do: basic grammar from the Nannul; silambam; an instrument or the parai; travel to the five landscapes; look at an inscription in a nearby temple; speech contests and debates.

Stage 4: higher (16 – 20 years)

  • Read: Kurunthokai and Purananuru (with commentary); the whole Tirukkural; Thiruvasagam, Thiruppavai, Tirumantiram; Siddhar songs; short stories of Pudhumaipithan, Jayakanthan and Ki. Rajanarayanan; essays of Periyar, Iyothee Thass and Thiru.Vi.Ka.; U.V. Swaminatha Iyer's En Charithram; Tho. Paramasivan's Panpattu Asaivugal.
  • Do: learn prosody and write a venba; record folk songs, stories and recipes from village elders; debate the history of social justice; write a technical article in Tamil.

Stage 5: lifelong learning

  • Read: the Porulathikaram of the Tolkappiyam; all of Sangam literature; the epics; modern novels; the literature of Eelam, the Hill Country, Malaysia and Singapore; at least ten new Tamil books a year.
  • Do: teach the children; run a library or reading circle; contribute to Wikipedia, Wikisource or Project Madurai; write new knowledge in Tamil.

What the community should do every year

  • Cook pongal on the first of Thai; keep the New Year in Chithirai; light lamps in Karthigai.
  • Desilt the village tank together (kudimaramathu).
  • Hold a yearly festival of literature, music and drama; a book fair.
  • Feed the guest; feed the hungry (the way of Vallalar).
  • Take common decisions by meeting and talking together; hold no one high or low by birth.

A measure across generations

How can one tell that a community has absorbed Tamil culture?

  • The children speak, read and write Tamil at home and at play.
  • Kurals and proverbs come naturally into conversation.
  • No guest is sent away unfed.
  • Pongal is a festival the whole village celebrates together.
  • "Every town is our town, everyone is our kin" and "All beings are equal by birth" are lived, not only quoted.
  • New science and new art are created in Tamil. Culture is not only guarding the old; it is going on creating.
